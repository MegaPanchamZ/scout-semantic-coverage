\section{TARGET DSL Extensions}
\label{app:dsl}

Table~\ref{tabA:dsl} formally defines the scenario DSL implemented in the
artifact. The DSL follows the modular DSL-to-scenario generation pattern
exemplified by TARGET~\cite{deng2025target}: a scenario program is compiled to
an internal \texttt{ScenarioSpec} and, where custom geometry is required, to an
OpenDRIVE map. The constructs below are the extensions added for semantic
coverage-guided testing (actor controllers, semantic targets, and the
parameterized five-dimensional search space).

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Scenario DSL constructs implemented in the artifact.}
\label{tabA:dsl}
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{2.3cm}L{4.2cm}L{5.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
Construct & Fields & Semantics \\
\midrule
\texttt{[environment]} & \texttt{town}, \texttt{weather}, \texttt{tick\_hz} & Simulation map, weather preset, and stepping rate (10\,Hz in this study). \\
\texttt{[ego]} & \texttt{route} & Ego route anchor (spawn and goal spawn-point indices); the ADS under test drives the ego. \\
\texttt{[[actor]]} & \texttt{type}, \texttt{spawn}, \texttt{controller}, \texttt{trigger}, \texttt{speed\_ms} & Adversarial actor with an explicit controller binding and a distance- or tick-based trigger. \\
\texttt{[targets]} & \texttt{obligations}, \texttt{hazard\_class} & Semantic targets for the coverage engine. Targets are metadata for generation; coverage credit always flows from observation. \\
\texttt{controller\_params} & \texttt{trigger\_radius\_m}, \texttt{speed}, \texttt{spawn\_transform}, \texttt{destination\_location}, \texttt{trigger\_tick} & Parameters mutated by the search: trigger radius, actor speed, lateral and longitudinal staging offsets, and release tick. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Hazard Templates}
\label{app:hazards}

The artifact defines nine hazard templates (Table~\ref{tabA:hazards}).
Templates are developer-defined conjunctions over the data-derived predicate
vocabulary; each template is instantiated automatically by matching it against
the nuScenes STSG corpus (Section~A.3), yielding the hazard obligations of the
semantic obligation inventory. Formulas follow the DoTA-style temporal
notation used in the inventory artifact where available.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{All nine hazard templates with formal definitions.}
\label{tabA:hazards}
\centering
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{2.2cm}L{4.3cm}L{5.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
Template & Semantic definition & Temporal formula (inventory artifact) \\
\midrule
other: ahead\_or\_waiting & vehicle actor that is in front of the ego and stationary for $\ge$ 2 consecutive keyframes & \texttt{Eventually(in\_front\_of(adversary, ego) AND (stationary(adversary) OR braking(adversary)))} \\
other: start\_stop\_or\_stationary & vehicle actor that is in front of the ego and drops $\ge$ 1.0 m/s of speed across the slice & \texttt{Eventually(in\_front\_of(adversary, ego) AND stationary(adversary))} \\
other: oncoming & vehicle actor with opposed heading in front of the ego that is closing distance & \texttt{Eventually(oncoming(adversary) AND approaching(adversary, ego))} \\
other: lateral & vehicle actor that changes to a laterally adjacent lane and crosses the ego path & \texttt{Eventually(lane\_changing(adversary) AND crossing\_path(adversary, ego))} \\
other: turning & vehicle actor that turns $\ge$ 30 deg while crossing the ego path & \texttt{Eventually(turning(adversary) AND crossing\_path(adversary, ego))} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{app:implementation}

\paragraph{Hazard instantiation.}
For each scene slice, the matcher first identifies graph nodes satisfying a
template's node predicates, then incrementally verifies the associated
attribute, relation, and temporal constraints. Every complete match corresponds
to one instantiated hazard obligation, recorded in the inventory with its
supporting slices. Constituent predicates are entirely data-derived; the
templates themselves are versioned and auditable.

\paragraph{Search algorithm.}
The search is an elitist hill-climber. A single candidate parameter vector is
evaluated per step; after the execution, the coverage engine updates the
suite-level obligation set. For the semantic policy, a candidate's fitness is
the number of previously uncovered obligations its execution closes (ties
broken by the total number of witnessed obligations; collisions are never
rewarded). The next candidate is a Gaussian mutation of the current elite
within the five-dimensional space; the random policy samples uniformly, and the
LSA and KMNC policies mutate around the elite under their respective fitness
values. Near-duplicate candidates are rejected before execution. The five
dimensions and bounds are: trigger radius $[5,35]$\,m, actor speed
$[0.8,4.0]$\,m/s, lateral staging offset $[1,14]$\,m, longitudinal staging
offset $[-10,30]$\,m, and trigger tick $[10,400]$ at 10\,Hz. The evaluated
scenario family implements the pedestrian-crossing hazard; per-template
parameter spaces for the remaining templates are not yet implemented and are
stated as a limitation in the main paper.

\paragraph{Coverage engine.}
The engine matches observer-emitted facts against the inventory through a
declarative crosswalk. Of the 103 trainval obligations, 78 (75.7\%) are
mapped, each labelled direct, derived, or proxy; unmapped obligations are
recorded with reasons, and four predicate types (\texttt{colliding},
\texttt{occluded}, \texttt{out\_of\_control}, \texttt{speeding}) are explicitly
ungrounded. Coverage is reported per dimension with first-witness ticks and
provenance; hazard conjunctions are credited only within a single trace.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Policy comparisons use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test over per-route paired
differences with Holm--Bonferroni correction across the three comparisons,
Vargha--Delaney $\hat{A}_{12}$ effect sizes, and bootstrap 95\% confidence
intervals (2{,}000 resamples, seed 0). Seeds are paired across policies per
route (seed 13).

Figure~3 of the TARGET paper~\cite{deng2025target} illustrates the modular
DSL-to-scenario pipeline that this work follows. For copyright reasons the
figure is not reproduced here; readers are referred to the original
publication.
